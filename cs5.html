05 · Met Office · Research in the Open (RITO) · 2025-2026

Building the infrastructure for research to actually land

Founded and led RITO, an org-wide ResearchOps initiative designed end-to-end using a UCD approach: discovery with 15 UCD professionals, journey mapping, How Might We workshops, and an MVP knowledge system now adopted by teams beyond the original portfolio.

Initiative Lead
15 UCD professionals
Service design, UCD
Org-wide adoption

The Challenge

Research findings that didn't survive the handoff

Across the Met Office's UCD community, the same pattern kept appearing: research was being done well, but the insights were not reliably making it into decisions. Findings lived in individual researchers' notes, in Confluence pages nobody visited, in presentations that were shared once and forgotten. The problem wasn't the quality of the research. It was the infrastructure around it.

I saw this as a solvable operations problem, not a cultural one. And I decided to approach solving it the same way I would approach any research problem: by starting with the people experiencing it.

The Approach

Applying UCD to UCD itself

Discovery interviews Journey mapping How Might We workshops MVP design and testing Community of practice facilitation

I ran discovery interviews with 15 UCD professionals across the organisation: researchers, designers, content designers, and accessibility specialists. I mapped the journey of a research insight from generation to decision, identifying where and why things were getting lost.

The How Might We workshops brought practitioners together to generate solutions they would actually use: not a system designed for them, but one designed with them. The MVP knowledge system that emerged was built around the real workflow, not an idealised version of it.

1

Discovery

15 interviews with UCD professionals across the organisation to understand how research currently moved, where it stalled, and what the real costs of that were.

2

Journey mapping

Mapped the research insight journey end-to-end, from raw data to decision. Identified 4 consistent drop-off points where insights were being lost.

3

How Might We workshops

Facilitated collaborative ideation sessions to generate solutions grounded in practitioner experience rather than theoretical best practice.

4

MVP build and test

Designed and tested an MVP knowledge system: a structured, navigable repository built around how people actually search for and apply research.

5

Adoption and handoff

The system was adopted by teams beyond the original portfolio. Documented the operating model so it could be maintained without depending on the founding researcher.

What I Found

The infrastructure problem was also a culture problem

Insight 01

Research was stored where it was made, not where it was needed

Insights lived in tools that made sense to researchers but not to the product managers and delivery managers who needed to act on them. The gap wasn't information. It was translation and accessibility.

Insight 02

No one owned the research after the researcher moved on

When researchers rotated between teams, their institutional knowledge left with them. The organisation was generating good research and then losing it. RITO created a system where ownership was structural, not personal.

Insight 03

Shared language made the system stick

The workshops didn't just generate ideas: they created a shared vocabulary for talking about research quality, research impact, and research debt. That shared language was what made adoption sustainable beyond the initial rollout.

What I'd do differently

I would involve delivery managers and product managers more heavily in the discovery phase. The system was designed primarily with researchers in mind. Its effectiveness as a handoff tool to other disciplines would have benefited from those disciplines being in the room earlier.
