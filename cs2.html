The Challenge

In aviation, a usability problem is a safety problem

MAVIS is used daily by pilots, glider instructors, drone operators, and operational meteorologists to make go/no-go flight decisions. Getting weather information wrong, or being unable to find it quickly, is not a usability problem. In aviation, it is a safety problem.

During observation of users in public beta, I noticed something not yet appearing in formal feedback: users were consistently taking longer than expected to locate the specific weather layers they needed. No one had complained. But the hesitation patterns were there: small, repeated moments of uncertainty in an environment where there is no margin for uncertainty.

The Approach

Qual and quant running in parallel

Behavioural observation Task-based usability testing Card sorting Cross-functional synthesis workshop GA4 path exploration Product telemetry

I chose card sorting over tree testing because the problem was not whether users could follow an existing structure: it was whether that structure made sense in the first place. Task-based usability testing allowed me to observe real behaviour under realistic conditions, capturing hesitation and backtracking that completion metrics alone would not have shown.

I also designed and ran a cross-functional synthesis workshop with product managers, designers, and developers: not just to share findings, but to move directly from user feedback to defined user needs to prioritised Jira tickets. This compressed weeks of potential back-and-forth into a single structured session.

The Evidence

GA4 path exploration made the navigation friction visible at scale

Starting from the map_layer_click event, path exploration showed 35,986 total interactions. At Step 2, 11,167 users triggered another map_layer_click immediately after the first: users cycling between layers because the structure did not match their mental model. Critically, 8,006 legend_click events followed map layer interactions, showing thousands of users going to the map key to decode what they were seeing.

What I Found

Three insights that changed the roadmap

Insight 01

Task completion masked the real problem

Users were technically completing tasks but relying on trial and error to do so. Standard completion metrics would have shown an acceptable pass rate. Observation revealed a service that was quietly dangerous under time pressure.

Insight 02

Quantitative data reversed a roadmap decision

Cloud Base had been deprioritised based on assumed demand. GA4 showed it was the most-used feature among general aviation users with 16,906 events in the first 28 days. Without that data, a safety-critical fix would have waited months.

Insight 03

Involving the team in synthesis accelerated delivery

Running a structured workshop where developers saw user evidence directly changed the tone of prioritisation conversations. Work that might have taken three sprints to agree was committed in a single session.

What I'd do differently

I would establish event tracking for individual map layer types from the start of the product lifecycle, not just tracking map_layer_click as a single event. Knowing which specific layers drove hesitation and legend lookups from day one would have made this analysis faster and more granular.
